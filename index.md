# Space Explorer

Space is the vast and mysterious expanse that exists beyond Earth's atmosphere. It contains everything from planets, stars, and galaxies to black holes and cosmic dust. For thousands of years, humans have looked up at the night sky with curiosity, wondering what lies beyond our world.

Today, space exploration allows scientists to study the universe using powerful telescopes, satellites, robotic probes, and crewed space missions.

## What is Space?

Outer space begins at about 100 kilometers above Earth's surface, a boundary known as the **Kármán line**. Beyond this point, Earth's atmosphere becomes extremely thin and spacecraft can orbit the planet.

Space is almost a perfect vacuum, meaning it contains very little matter. Because of this, sound cannot travel in space and temperatures can vary dramatically depending on exposure to the Sun.

---

## The Scale of the Universe

The universe is unimaginably large. Our home planet Earth is part of the **Solar System**, which contains eight planets orbiting the Sun. The Sun itself is just one of hundreds of billions of stars in our galaxy, the **Milky Way**.

Beyond our galaxy, scientists estimate that there are **billions of other galaxies**, each containing millions or billions of stars.

Some key facts about the universe:

- The universe is approximately **13.86 billion years old**
- The observable universe contains **over 100 billion galaxies**
- Light from distant galaxies can take **millions or billions of years** to reach Earth

---

## Why Space Exploration Matters

Space exploration has led to many important scientific discoveries and technological advancements. Studying space helps us better understand the origins of the universe, the formation of planets, and the possibility of life beyond Earth.

Technologies developed for space missions have also improved life on Earth, including:

- satellite communication
- weather forecasting
- GPS navigation
- advanced materials and medical technologies
