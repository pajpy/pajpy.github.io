## The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system has **eight planets**. The first four — Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars — are rocky terrestrial planets, while Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune are giant outer planets. 

This page presents the main facts about each planet, together with a few interesting details that make every world unique.

---

## Mercury

![Mercury](assets/images/mercury.jfif)

Mercury is the **closest planet to the Sun** and also the **smallest planet** in the solar system. It moves around the Sun faster than any other planet, completing one orbit in just **88 Earth days**. Mercury has no moons and almost no atmosphere, which is why its surface temperatures change dramatically between day and night. 

### Unique features

- Mercury has the **largest temperature range** of any planet in the solar system.
- Despite being closest to the Sun, it is **not the hottest planet**.
- A single day on Mercury (sunrise to sunrise) lasts **176 Earth days**.

### Interesting facts
- Mercury is **not** the hottest planet, even though it is closest to the Sun; Venus is hotter because of its dense atmosphere.
- Daytime temperatures can reach about **430°C**, while nighttime temperatures can drop to about **-180°C**.

## Venus

![Venus](assets/images/venus.jfif)

Venus is the **second planet from the Sun** and Earth’s closest planetary neighbor. It is similar to Earth in size, which is why it is sometimes called Earth’s twin, but its environment is extremely hostile. Its thick atmosphere causes a **runaway greenhouse effect**, making Venus the **hottest planet in the solar system**. 

### Unique features

- Venus rotates **backwards** compared to most planets.
- Its thick clouds reflect sunlight, making it the **brightest planet visible from Earth**.
- A day on Venus is actually **longer than its year**.

### Interesting facts
- Venus rotates very slowly.
- Beneath its thick clouds, Venus has volcanoes, mountains, and a rocky surface shaped by intense heat and pressure. 

---

## Earth

![Earth](assets/images/earth.jfif)

Earth is the **third planet from the Sun** and the **only world known to support life**. It is the fifth largest planet in the solar system and the largest of the four rocky inner planets. Liquid water covers much of Earth’s surface, and its atmosphere and temperature make it suitable for life. 

### Unique features

- Earth has a **strong magnetic field** that protects life from harmful solar radiation.
- About **71% of Earth's surface is covered by water**.
- Earth is the only known planet with **active plate tectonics**.

### Interesting facts
- Earth is the only planet in the solar system whose English name does **not** come from Greek or Roman mythology. 
- A day on Earth lasts about **23.9 hours**, and a year lasts about **365.25 days**. 

---

## Mars

![Mars](assets/images/mars.jfif)

Mars is the **fourth planet from the Sun**. It is a cold, dusty desert world with a very thin atmosphere. Mars has seasons, polar ice caps, giant volcanoes, deep canyons, and weather, which makes it one of the most studied planets in the solar system. 

### Unique features

- Mars is home to **Olympus Mons**, the largest volcano in the solar system.
- It also contains **Valles Marineris**, one of the largest canyon systems known.
- Evidence suggests that **liquid water existed on Mars in the past**.

### Interesting facts
- Mars is often called the **Red Planet** because iron minerals in its soil rust and give the surface a reddish color.
- NASA describes Mars as the only planet we know that is currently **inhabited entirely by robots**. 

---

## Jupiter

![Jupiter](assets/images/jupiter.jfif)

Jupiter is the **largest planet** in the solar system. It is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium, and it has a very powerful atmosphere with cloud bands, storms, and strong winds. Jupiter also has a faint ring system and many moons. 

### Unique features

- Jupiter has the **Great Red Spot**, a storm that has lasted for hundreds of years.
- The planet has a **very strong magnetic field**, the strongest of any planet.
- Jupiter’s moon **Ganymede** is the largest moon in the solar system.

### Interesting facts
- Jupiter’s **Great Red Spot** is a giant storm that is bigger than Earth. 
- Jupiter has the **shortest day** of any planet, taking about **9.9 hours** to rotate once. 

---

## Saturn

![Saturn](assets/images/saturn.jfif)

Saturn is the **sixth planet from the Sun** and the **second largest** in the solar system. It is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium and is best known for its spectacular ring system. 

### Unique features

- Saturn’s rings are made mostly of **ice and rock particles**.
- Saturn is so light that it would **float in water if there were an ocean large enough**.
- One of its moons, **Titan**, has lakes and rivers of liquid methane.

### Interesting facts
- Saturn’s rings are the most prominent in the solar system, even though all four giant planets have rings. 
- A day on Saturn lasts only about **10.7 hours**, while one year lasts about **29.4 Earth years**. 

---

## Uranus

![Uranus](assets/images/uranus.jfif)

Uranus is the **seventh planet from the Sun** and the **third largest** planet in the solar system. It is an ice giant and is known for its unusual rotation. The planet is very cold, windy, and surrounded by faint rings and many small moons.

### Unique features

- Uranus rotates **on its side**, unlike any other planet.
- Because of this tilt, its seasons last about **21 Earth years**.
- Its blue color comes from **methane gas in the atmosphere**.

### Interesting facts
- Uranus rotates at a nearly **90-degree angle**, so it appears to spin on its side. 
- This unusual tilt makes Uranus one of the most distinctive planets in the solar system. 

---

## Neptune

![Neptune](assets/images/neptune.jfif)

Neptune is the **eighth and most distant planet** in the solar system. It is a dark, cold ice giant with powerful winds and a deep blue appearance. Neptune is more than 30 times farther from the Sun than Earth and cannot be seen with the naked eye. 

### Unique features

- Neptune has the **strongest winds in the solar system**, reaching over 2,000 km/h.
- It was the **first planet discovered using mathematical predictions**.
- Its moon **Triton** orbits the planet in the opposite direction of Neptune’s rotation.

### Interesting facts
- Neptune was the **first planet discovered through mathematical prediction** rather than direct observation alone.
- It takes Neptune about **165 Earth years** to orbit the Sun once.
